Source
Open problem. Whether an odd perfect number exists is unknown. This project does not claim a proof; it formalizes necessary conditions.

Milestones

Each entry is a verified commit on the main branch, oldest first, generated from the git history. Times are local (UTC−5); the date is shown when it changes.

Classes: known result formalized a known result, proved again in Lean. restricted exclusion or bound checked a new or sharpened statement under restrictive hypotheses. changes the general case a result that constrains every odd perfect number in a way that moves toward a resolution. The housekeeping commit carries no class.

No milestone so far changes the status of the conjecture. None is in the third class.

  1. 20 November 2026, 20:08

    Project setup. 06daf26 known result formalized

    The Lean project with mathlib pinned, the two definitions in Basic, and eight seed modules. They prove the forcing step and valuation conservation for exact prime power components of any perfect number, the coprime cut lemma, an exclusion for exponents ≡ 2 (mod 3) at both 3 and 5, residue facts for exact squares when 3 does not divide N, and partial results and numerical checks on collisions of x² + x + 1.

  2. 20:19

    Foundations. 723cd0d known result formalized

    Euler's form N = qkm², from the 2-adic ledger, and Touchard's congruence. Abundancy as an exact rational, with at least three distinct prime factors. The cyclotomic factorization of σ(pa), the order dichotomy for its prime divisors, and exact valuations by lifting the exponent. The valuation network with conservation and strong connectivity.

  3. 20:42

    First structural wave. a3fb806 known result formalized

    Twelve modules. The closed loop exclusion and the bound 3qk < 2m²; exact squares at every ordinary prime are impossible when 3 divides N; at least four distinct prime factors; primitive prime divisors for bases at least 3; bounds on the smallest prime factor; refined congruences and small abundant divisors; linear inequalities between Ω and ω; arithmetic of x² + x + 1; the certificate checker and Pratt primality certificates; the Descartes spoof; and the equivalence of the conjecture with the nonexistence of a balanced network.

  4. 20:51

    Exponent patterns. 6b837aa known result formalized

    Steuerwald's theorem, with the prime length generalization for exponents l − 1 when l does not divide N; hence some prime has an even exponent at least 4 and m in Euler's form is not squarefree. McDaniel's theorem, by a forcing tree with abundant divisor leaves when 3 divides N and by a geometric growth argument otherwise.

  5. 20:56

    Housekeeping. 931dd10

    A contributor note was removed from the repository. No Lean file changed.

  6. 21:04

    Collision gap. 12d8877 restricted exclusion or bound checked

    If x² + x + 1 = pb+2kr and y² + y + 1 = pbr with y ≥ 3, then p2br < pk, so k > 2b. The proof parametrizes x² + x + 1 = K²(y² + y + 1) for general K and compares sizes.

  7. 21:05

    Upper bound. c338dec known result formalized

    An odd N with dσ(N) = nN and k distinct prime factors satisfies dN < (d + 1)4k; for an odd perfect number this bounds N by its number of distinct prime factors alone. The proof is an induction driven by a lemma on products of (1 − 1/x).

  8. 21:12

    Five prime factors. 76490a7 known result formalized

    The case of exactly four prime factors reduces, by abundancy bounds and exponent forcing, to 104 explicit supports, each closed by a kernel computation on component divisor sums.

  9. 21:14

    Square pattern certificates. 27177b0 restricted exclusion or bound checked

    Factor chain certificates checked in the kernel exclude exponent 2 at 5, 11, 29 and x for x ∈ {17, 23, 47} outright, and for x ∈ {41, 53} when 3 does not divide N.

  10. 21:16

    Exact valuations. 4a219f4 known result formalized

    Closed forms for the q-adic valuation of σ(pa) for odd q of any order, for q = 2, and for q = 3 at every exponent, with the resulting 2-adic and 3-adic ledgers of a perfect number.

  11. 21:17

    One exception forces 3. e96056b restricted exclusion or bound checked

    If every prime other than the special prime and one further prime r has exponent 2, then 3 divides N. The proof is elementary: abundancy bounds from the shape σ(p²) = qarb, with no Diophantine input. Novelty unverified.

  12. 21:21

    Exponent caps and finiteness. 21d4c54 known result formalized

    Every exponent e satisfies 2e + 3 ≤ R for the largest prime factor R; the divisors of e + 1 inject into the other prime factors; for each number of distinct prime factors, and for each bound on the prime factors, only finitely many odd perfect numbers can exist. Without 3, the special prime is ≡ 1 (mod 12).

  13. 21:24

    All exponents four. b064f5b known result formalized

    If 5 does not divide N the prime length argument applies. If 5 is ordinary, the forced primes 11, 71, 211 and their successors supply more than four factors of 5. If 5 is the special prime, the 3-adic ledger forces 6 to divide k + 1, so 7 divides N and 3⁴·5·7⁴ is abundant.

  14. 21:34

    Counting inequalities. 09f832c known result formalized

    Both inequalities follow from one ledger of the exponent 2 primes and two injections, using an elementary proof that 3(x² + x + 1) is never y² + y + 1 for an odd prime x.

  15. 21:54

    Quadratic residues and exponent six. fa5d60b restricted exclusion or bound checked

    σ(pa) ≡ 1 (mod p) gives one sign identity per vertex of the network; a prime dividing σ(pe) with e even has p as a quadratic residue; two prime factors that are mutual quadratic residues always exist (novelty unverified). Exponent six: forcing seven primes ≡ 1 (mod 7) against a 7-adic budget of six.

  16. 22:11

    Four modules from the verification queue. c89057f known result formalized

    Integer spoof factorizations, with a signed example checked against each library condition separately, and positivity lemmas that separate genuine numbers from signed spoofs. For equal ordinary exponents: an l-adic counting bound, divisibility by l when every ordinary e + 1 is a power of l, and l⁴ dividing N. Identities for the index of Euler's form, congruences modulo 8, and an exact identity linking abundancy to the radical, with linear bounds on the second, third and fourth smallest prime factors.

  17. 22:16

    Valuation capacity and edge orders. 1fa9401 restricted exclusion or bound checked

    With k distinct prime factors, the exponent of an odd prime q is at most (k − 1)² plus order terms from the other components, with sharper forms when q − 1 is a power of two and for q = 3. An edge from a smaller prime to a larger one has multiplicative order at least 3, and if the largest prime factor P has even exponent e then 2e + 5 ≤ P. A formal version of the largest prime condition fails for the Descartes spoof.

  18. 22:24

    Algebra of x² + x + 1. 6a645c4 known result formalized

    The new module Phi3Algebra collects identities such as φ₃(b²) = φ₃(b)(b² − b + 1), proved by ring over the integers, and solves φ₃(x) = m·φ₃(b) for prime φ₃(b) by comparing two roots of t² + t + 1 modulo that prime. It also shows that x² + x + 1 has at most two roots modulo a prime and is never a cube at a prime x. These are the algebraic inputs of the inequalities between Ω and ω.

  19. 22:35

    Size bounds and restricted exceptions. 5c81b41 restricted exclusion or bound checked

    The new module AttackSize combines exact arithmetic with size. Primes whose σ(p²) is a power of the special prime are shown to be at least 59 and widely spaced, which forces 3 to divide N when every ordinary exponent is 2 apart from a restricted set of exceptions. A set S of prime factors all at least p, not containing every prime factor, gives 2p2|S| < 3N through an edge of the network into S.

  20. 22:45

    Upper bounds for prime factors. 6d4893b restricted exclusion or bound checked

    The new module LargePrimes bounds prime factors from above in terms of N. Its basic step is that pe and σ(pe) are coprime divisors of 2N, which gives p⁴ < 2N for every exponent at least 2; combining two components gives p⁴q < 2N for p < q and (pq)⁴ < 6N². A separate argument for exponent 1 gives p³ < 3N for every prime factor.

  21. 22:57

    Mutually dividing pairs for x² + x + 1. d9b08f3 known result formalized

    The new module VietaPairs classifies the pairs p ≤ q with p | q² + q + 1 and q | p² + p + 1: they are the consecutive terms of 1, 1, 3, 13, 61, 291, …, and all satisfy p² + q² + p + q + 1 = 5pq. The proof descends from (p, q) to ((p² + p + 1)/q, p) on the divisibility relations themselves. Residues and growth of the sequence give consequences for primes with exponent 2 that feed each other.
