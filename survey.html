Source
Open problem. Whether an odd perfect number exists is unknown. This project does not claim a proof; it formalizes necessary conditions.

Literature and code survey

The survey feeds the list of targets and guards against repeating known mistakes. Aggregate numbers are reported here; the screened papers are listed in the survey register.

Papers

287 arXiv papers on perfect numbers and related topics were screened by title and abstract. 131 were graded as directly relevant or transferable and read in full from their sources, each with a written note.

Repositories

101 public repositories matching the topic were reviewed by reading only. A handful contain useful certificates or lemma statements, which were reproved here rather than copied.

Claimed proofs

Sixteen papers that claim to settle the conjecture or a related open statement were read as a referee would. In each case our reading found a step that does not follow. These readings have not been externally reviewed.

What the survey produced

Target statements for the modules of the library, a list of results still missing from it, and the countermodel checklist used to test new ideas (see Why it is hard).

The survey register lists the 287 screened papers with their grades; the 131 papers read in full are those graded A or B. The reading notes, the list of reviewed repositories and the sixteen readings of claimed proofs are not published, so those two counts cannot be checked from public material.

Papers and third party code are read as data only: nothing from them is executed or copied into the library, and every borrowed statement is proved again in Lean (see the trust policy).
