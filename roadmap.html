Source
Open problem. Whether an odd perfect number exists is unknown. This project does not claim a proof; it formalizes necessary conditions.

Status

One table for the whole project. The conjecture that no odd perfect number exists is open, and nothing below decides it. Every row names the parameter that stays unbounded.

Three labels, nothing else. Formal theorem: on the main branch, checked by the Lean kernel, with the three standard axioms only. Paper proof awaiting formalization: an argument checked by hand, not yet compiled; it may still fail. Research target: no proof is known to us. A named proposition in the library is not a proof of that proposition.

Notation

An odd perfect number has the form N = qa m2 with q prime, q not dividing m, and q and a both 1 modulo 4 (Euler). The prime q is the special prime; the other primes are ordinary and have even exponents. ω is the number of distinct prime factors and Ω the number counted with multiplicity. The excess is T = ∑ (ep/2 − 1) over the ordinary primes p, so that Ω = a + 2(ω − 1) + 2T.

What no row reaches

  • An upper bound for the excess T, or the exclusion of any T above 6.
  • An upper bound for the special exponent a, or the inequality qa < m.
  • An upper bound for v3(N) in the restrained family; lower bounds alone cannot close it.
  • Three or more exceptions beyond divisibility by 3; common index families with mixed rows.

These are attempts on an open problem. The expected outcome is further partial results, not a resolution. A row labeled as a paper proof moves to the milestones only when they land on the main branch after the full build.
