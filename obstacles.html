Source
Open problem. Whether an odd perfect number exists is unknown. This project does not claim a proof; it formalizes necessary conditions.

Why the problem resists

Some objects satisfy a precisely stated relaxation of the problem without being odd perfect numbers. Each one refutes a specific implication: the relaxed assumptions alone do not give a contradiction. None of them is a statement about every possible proof.

The Descartes spoof is in the library as a formal test (Spoof), and the signed spoof is in SpoofInt. Both are tagged as tools: they are test cases, not results about odd perfect numbers.

Controls for a proposed argument

A proposed proof of nonexistence is checked against each of these controls. An argument that also applies to one of them proves too much.

  1. Descartes' spoof 3²·7²·11²·13²·22021. Exposes a hidden assumption that every base is prime.
  2. Voight's signed spoof 3⁴·7²·11²·19²·(−127). Exposes an argument that never uses positivity.
  3. The genuine prime two cycle 13 ↔ 61 for x² + x + 1. 61 divides 13² + 13 + 1 = 183 = 3·61 and 13 divides 61² + 61 + 1 = 3783 = 3·13·97. So no potential can strictly increase along every edge of the network.
  4. σ(3⁴) = 121 = 11². One edge can carry weight two.
  5. P = 19 receives from both 7 and 11 with order three. σ(7²) = 57 = 3·19 and σ(11²) = 133 = 7·19, and both 7 and 11 have order 3 modulo 19. Primitive prime witnesses are therefore not injective across rows.
  6. A feasible integer assignment to the exported counting constraints with ω = 119 and Ω = 315 in the case 3 ∤ N. It shows that the exported linear constraints alone cannot give the sharper counting inequality of Clayton and Hansen. It is an assignment to the constraints, not a number N. The inequality itself is now proved in CountingClaytonHansen, with further relations.

The global quantifier. The conjecture quantifies over arbitrary finite supports and unbounded exponents jointly. Results at fixed ω, at a fixed largest prime, or up to a finite cutoff do not settle the conjecture, however many cases they close.

The working direction

This project investigates arguments that combine primality of every node of the network, positivity and size, the 2-adic budget, finiteness of the support, and exact equality. The examples above show why certain simpler relaxations are insufficient. This is a research preference, not a theorem about the shape of every proof: a proof could proceed through several intermediate contradictions or through a different invariant. The theorems that combine exact structure with inequalities, such as the one exception theorem, are small instances of that shape.
