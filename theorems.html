A selection from the library. Throughout, N is an odd perfect number, q is its special prime (the one prime with an odd exponent), and the other primes are called ordinary. ω counts distinct prime factors and Ω counts prime factors with multiplicity. Statements are copied from the source by the site generator; names are the Lean identifiers.

Classes: known result formalized a known result, proved again in Lean. restricted exclusion or bound checked a new or sharpened statement under restrictive hypotheses. changes the general case a result that constrains every odd perfect number in a way that moves toward a resolution; nothing in the library is in this class beyond classical theorems, which are counted as known results. Tags: novelty unverified proved here and not located in the literature by us; no priority is claimed. tool infrastructure used by other proofs, without a class.

Euler's form

known result formalized

N = qkm² with q prime, q not dividing m, and q ≡ k ≡ 1 (mod 4).

theorem exists_euler_form (h : OddPerfect N) :
  ∃ q k m : ℕ, q.Prime ∧ N = q ^ k * m ^ 2 ∧ ¬ q ∣ m ∧ q % 4 = 1 ∧ k % 4 = 1

OddPerfect.exists_euler_form in OddPerfect.Euler

Proof idea. The 2-adic ledger. N is odd, so σ(N) = 2N has exactly one factor 2, shared among the components σ(pe). For odd p, σ(pe) has e + 1 odd terms, so it is even exactly when e is odd: one prime q has odd exponent k, and v₂(σ(qk)) = 1. If q ≡ 3 (mod 4), pairing consecutive terms puts q + 1, a multiple of 4, into σ(qk). So q ≡ 1 (mod 4), then σ(qk) ≡ k + 1 (mod 4) forces k ≡ 1 (mod 4).

Touchard's congruence, refined

known result formalized

N ≡ 1 (mod 12), or N ≡ 117 (mod 468), or N ≡ 81 (mod 324).

theorem mod_refined (h : OddPerfect N) :
  N % 12 = 1 ∨ N % 468 = 117 ∨ N % 324 = 81

OddPerfect.mod_refined in OddPerfect.SpecialPrime

Proof idea. From N ≡ 1 (mod 4) and Touchard's N ≡ 1 (mod 12) or N ≡ 9 (mod 36). If 3 divides N, its exponent is even. If it is exactly 2, then σ(3²) = 13 divides N, and N is a multiple of 9·13 = 117 that is ≡ 1 (mod 4), so N ≡ 117 (mod 468). Otherwise 3⁴ divides N and N ≡ 81 (mod 324).

At least six prime factors

known result formalized

ω(N) ≥ 6. Without the prime 3 at least eight, and without 3 and 5 at least fifteen. The step from five to six is a search over the prime factors in increasing order, run by the kernel with a soundness proof; it is described in the module OmegaSix.

theorem six_le_card_primeFactors {N : ℕ} (h : OddPerfect N) : 6 ≤ N.primeFactors.card

OddPerfect.six_le_card_primeFactors in OddPerfect.OmegaSix

The bound ω(N) ≥ 7 is not proved here. For exactly six prime factors the library has partial results: 15 divides N and the third smallest prime factor lies between 11 and 31 (module OmegaSeven).

theorem exists_prime_dvd_of_card_eq_six {N : ℕ} (h : OddPerfect N)
  (h6 : N.primeFactors.card = 6) :
  3 ∣ N ∧ 5 ∣ N ∧ ∃ p, p.Prime ∧ 11 ≤ p ∧ p ≤ 31 ∧ p ∣ N

OddPerfect.exists_prime_dvd_of_card_eq_six in OddPerfect.OmegaSeven

The earlier bound ω(N) ≥ 5 has a separate proof:

theorem five_le_card_primeFactors {N : ℕ} (h : OddPerfect N) : 5 ≤ N.primeFactors.card

OddPerfect.five_le_card_primeFactors in OddPerfect.OmegaFive

Proof idea. With four prime factors the product bound 2 < ∏ p/(p − 1) leaves 104 explicit supports. For each one a kernel computation lists the possible exponents of each prime and shows that some σ(xe) always has a prime factor outside the support.

Also eight_le_card_primeFactors_of_not_three_dvd and fifteen_le_card_primeFactors_of_not_three_dvd_of_not_five_dvd.

Upper bound

known result formalized

N is smaller than 2 to the power 4ω(N). Hence there are finitely many candidates for each ω.

theorem lt_two_pow_four_pow {N : ℕ} (h : OddPerfect N) :
  N < 2 ^ (4 ^ N.primeFactors.card)

OddPerfect.lt_two_pow_four_pow in OddPerfect.UpperBound

Proof idea. An induction on the number of prime factors, driven by a lemma on products of (1 − 1/x) over increasing integers: at each step a coprime factor V of N is split off and the cofactor still has rational abundancy, with a controlled denominator.

Equal exponent patterns

known result formalized

The ordinary exponents cannot all be 2, all be 4, 6, 10, 12, 16, 22 or 28, or all be congruent to 2 modulo 3. The first is Steuerwald's theorem, the last McDaniel's; the others are due to Kanold (4), Hagis and McDaniel (6, 10) and Cohen and Williams (12, 16, 22, 28). The library also excludes the common exponent 18, for which we did not find a published proof (novelty unverified; an independent computer assisted claim by another author exists and is not peer reviewed). The module EqualExponentChainMore adds the common exponents 30, 36, 40 and 42. The exponent 36 is due to Cohen and Williams; for 30, 40 and 42 we did not find a published proof (novelty unverified). The common exponent 24 (Hagis and McDaniel, 1975), whose length 25 is not prime, has its own proof in ExponentTwentyFour. The module CommonPrimeChain excludes every common exponent E with 5 dividing E + 1 and E < 186, and with 7 dividing E + 1 and E < 124; more generally, if 5 divides every ordinary e + 1 then 5186 divides N, and for 7 the bound is 7124. Of these exponents, 34 and 48 are due to Hagis and McDaniel. A preprint of 2015 claims that the family of 5 is impossible altogether, by a far larger computation; for the family of 7 we did not find published statements (novelty unverified). So every even common exponent up to 44 is excluded; 46 is the first that is not. One family is excluded as a whole (module CommonFamily): if 35 divides every ordinary e + 1, there is no odd perfect number (Hagis and McDaniel). The same holds for 65 (Evans and Pearlman; module CommonSixtyFive) and for 55, 85 and 115 (modules CommonFiftyFive, CommonEightyFive and CommonOneFifteen; for these three we did not locate a published proof, novelty unverified). In the family of 5, neither 3 nor 7 divides N and the special prime is ≡ 1 (mod 12) (Evans and Pearlman; modules CommonFamily and CommonFiveSeven). More generally, if t divides e + 1 for every ordinary exponent e, then t⁴ divides N. If t is a power of an ordinary prime, then t⁵ divides N; for a power qa of the special prime this is proved when a is not 2 (module KanoldFive).

theorem steuerwald {N q : ℕ} (h : OddPerfect N) (hq : q.Prime)
  (hodd : Odd (N.factorization q))
  (hsq : ∀ p ∈ N.primeFactors, p ≠ q → N.factorization p = 2) : False

OddPerfect.steuerwald in OddPerfect.Steuerwald

Proof idea. If 3 does not divide N, every σ(p²) of an ordinary p is a power of the special prime, which the closed loop argument excludes. If 3 divides N, the 3-adic budget is exactly 2, and a forcing chain from σ(3²) = 13 produces three ordinary primes ≡ 1 (mod 3), each adding a factor 3.

Also mcdaniel, not_all_exponents_four, not_all_exponents_six, not_all_exponents_ten, not_all_exponents_twelve, the certificate checker of EqualExponentChain for the exponents listed above, and pow_four_dvd_of_dvd_succ, explained in their modules on the Reference page.

Small primes cannot all be present

known result formalized

105 does not divide N. The smallest prime factor p satisfies 3p < 2ω + 6.

theorem not_105_dvd (h : OddPerfect N) : ¬ 105 ∣ N

OddPerfect.not_105_dvd in OddPerfect.SpecialPrime

Proof idea. The primes 3 and 7 are ≡ 3 (mod 4), so they cannot be special and appear at least squared. Then 3²·5·7² divides N, and σ(3²·5·7²) = 13·6·57 = 4446 exceeds 2·2205 = 4410: an abundant divisor, impossible for a perfect number.

theorem three_mul_minFac_lt {N : ℕ} (h : OddPerfect N) :
  3 * N.minFac < 2 * N.primeFactors.card + 6

OddPerfect.three_mul_minFac_lt in OddPerfect.SmallestPrime

Proof idea. The product of p/(p − 1) over the prime factors exceeds 2. Over odd numbers at least p₁, its square telescopes to at most 1 + 2ω/(p₁ − 2). So 4 < 1 + 2ω/(p₁ − 2), which rearranges to 3p₁ < 2ω + 6.

Counting inequalities

known result formalized

Total and distinct prime factor counts are linked, after Zelinsky (arXiv 1706.07009): 8ω ≤ 3Ω + 7 when 3 does not divide N, and 21ω ≤ 8Ω + 39 in general.

theorem eight_mul_omega_le_of_not_three_dvd {N : ℕ} (h : OddPerfect N) (h3 : ¬ 3 ∣ N) :
  8 * ω N ≤ 3 * Ω N + 7

OddPerfect.eight_mul_omega_le_of_not_three_dvd in OddPerfect.CountingZelinsky

theorem twentyone_mul_omega_le {N : ℕ} (h : OddPerfect N) : 21 * ω N ≤ 8 * Ω N + 39

OddPerfect.twentyone_mul_omega_le in OddPerfect.CountingZelinsky

Proof idea. A ledger of the exponent 2 primes and their divisor sums σ(p²), with conservation at the receiving primes, plus two injections: the largest prime factor of σ(p²), and the 3-free part of σ(p²) when it is prime, each determine p.

The sharper inequalities of Clayton and Hansen (arXiv 2303.11974) are also proved: 99ω ≤ 37Ω + 187 in general, and 51ω ≤ 19Ω + 46 when 3 does not divide N. The proof is outlined in the module CountingClaytonHansen.

theorem ninetynine_mul_omega_le {N : ℕ} (h : OddPerfect N) : 99 * ω N ≤ 37 * Ω N + 187

OddPerfect.ninetynine_mul_omega_le in OddPerfect.CountingClaytonHansen

theorem fiftyone_mul_omega_le_of_not_three_dvd {N : ℕ} (h : OddPerfect N) (h3 : ¬ 3 ∣ N) :
  51 * ω N ≤ 19 * Ω N + 46

OddPerfect.fiftyone_mul_omega_le_of_not_three_dvd in OddPerfect.CountingClaytonHansen

The special prime power is small

known result formalized

In Euler's form, 3qk < 2m².

theorem special_pow_lt_sq {N q k m : ℕ} (h : OddPerfect N) (hq : q.Prime)
  (hN : N = q ^ k * m ^ 2) (hqm : ¬ q ∣ m) (hk : Odd k) : 3 * q ^ k < 2 * m ^ 2

OddPerfect.special_pow_lt_sq in OddPerfect.ClosedLoop

Proof idea. From σ(qk)σ(m²) = 2qkm² and q not dividing σ(qk), write σ(m²) = qkt, so σ(qk)t = 2m². Here t is odd (σ(qk) is even and m is odd), and t ≠ 1 because σ(m²) = qk is excluded by the closed loop argument. So t ≥ 3 and 3qk < 3σ(qk) ≤ 2m².

One exception forces 3

restricted exclusion or bound checked novelty unverified

If every prime other than q and one further prime r has exponent exactly 2, then 3 divides N. The proof uses elementary arithmetic and abundancy bounds, without a Diophantine classification or an external search. The setting is that of Ochem and Zelinsky (arXiv 2607.19746). Whether this route is new has not been checked.

theorem three_dvd_of_one_exception {N q r : ℕ} (h : OddPerfect N) (hq : q.Prime)
  (hodd : Odd (N.factorization q)) (hr : r.Prime) (hrq : r ≠ q)
  (hsq : ∀ p ∈ N.primeFactors, p ≠ q → p ≠ r → N.factorization p = 2) : 3 ∣ N

OddPerfect.three_dvd_of_one_exception in OddPerfect.ThreeFreeException

Proof idea. Assume 3 does not divide N. Then q ≡ 1 (mod 12), and for every other ordinary p, σ(p²) = qarb. Size comparisons on these values, split by whether r divides some σ(p²), bound the abundancy below 2. No computer search is used.

The same conclusion holds with two exceptional primes r₁ and r₂, with no condition on them (module ThreeFreeTwo). Whether this statement is new has not been checked.

theorem three_dvd_of_two_exceptions {N q r₁ r₂ : ℕ} (h : OddPerfect N) (hq : q.Prime)
  (hodd : Odd (N.factorization q))
  (hsq : ∀ p ∈ N.primeFactors, p ≠ q → p ≠ r₁ → p ≠ r₂ → N.factorization p = 2) :
  3 ∣ N

OddPerfect.three_dvd_of_two_exceptions in OddPerfect.ThreeFreeTwo

Mutual quadratic residues

restricted exclusion or bound checked novelty unverified

N always has two prime factors that are quadratic residues of each other. This removes a congruence hypothesis from a theorem of Wallace (arXiv 1610.00544). Whether the stronger form is new has not been checked.

theorem exists_mutual_quadratic_residues {N : ℕ} (h : OddPerfect N) :
  ∃ p ∈ N.primeFactors, ∃ r ∈ N.primeFactors, p ≠ r ∧
    jacobiSym p r = 1 ∧ jacobiSym r p = 1

OddPerfect.exists_mutual_quadratic_residues in OddPerfect.Reciprocity

Proof idea. The special prime q has positive exponent, so by conservation some prime p ≠ q sends weight to q. Its exponent e is even, so pe+1 ≡ 1 (mod q) with e + 1 odd, which makes p a square modulo q. Since q ≡ 1 (mod 4), quadratic reciprocity makes q a square modulo p.

The largest prime factor

restricted exclusion or bound checked novelty unverified

The largest prime factor P is not of the form 3·4t + 1 with t ≥ 2, and P − 1 is not a power of 2. The first statement covers every t ≥ 2 at once. The proof looks at the multiplicative orders of the primes that send a factor P; it is outlined in the module LargestPrimeOrder. Whether these exclusions are new has not been checked.

theorem largest_prime_ne_three_mul_four_pow {N P t : ℕ} (h : OddPerfect N)
  (hP : P ∈ N.primeFactors) (hmax : ∀ r ∈ N.primeFactors, r ≤ P) (ht : 2 ≤ t) :
  P ≠ 3 * 4 ^ t + 1

OddPerfect.largest_prime_ne_three_mul_four_pow in OddPerfect.LargestPrimeOrder

theorem largest_prime_sub_one_ne_two_pow (h : OddPerfect N) {P : ℕ}
  (hP : P ∈ N.primeFactors) (hmax : ∀ r ∈ N.primeFactors, r ≤ P) (j : ℕ) :
  P - 1 ≠ 2 ^ j

OddPerfect.largest_prime_sub_one_ne_two_pow in OddPerfect.LargestPrimeOrder

Both statements are cases of a capacity bound: with m the odd part of P − 1, the exponent of P is at most the sum of φ(d)(φ(d) − 1) over the divisors d > 1 of m (module LargestPrimeCapacity). For large m this is weaker than the general exponent cap below.

theorem factorization_largest_le_sum_totient (h : OddPerfect N) {P a m : ℕ}
  (hP : P ∈ N.primeFactors) (hmax : ∀ r ∈ N.primeFactors, r ≤ P)
  (hm : P - 1 = 2 ^ a * m) (hmo : Odd m) :
  N.factorization P ≤ ∑ d ∈ m.divisors.erase 1, d.totient * (d.totient - 1)

OddPerfect.factorization_largest_le_sum_totient in OddPerfect.LargestPrimeCapacity

Every odd perfect number exceeds 1079 (module LowerBound); much larger bounds are known in the literature.

theorem lower_bound {N : ℕ} (h : OddPerfect N) : 10 ^ 79 < N

OddPerfect.LowerBound.lower_bound in OddPerfect.LowerBound

Separately, some prime factor exceeds 10000. This is a known type of result, and much larger bounds are in the literature; here a search with a soundness proof is run by the kernel (module LargestPrimeFactor).

theorem exists_primeFactor_gt {N : ℕ} (h : OddPerfect N) : ∃ p ∈ N.primeFactors, 10000 < p

OddPerfect.exists_primeFactor_gt in OddPerfect.LargestPrimeFactor

Two distinct prime factors exceed 58 (module SecondLargestPrime). Much larger bounds for the second largest prime factor are in the literature; this is the range the kernel checks here.

theorem exists_two_primeFactors_gt {N : ℕ} (h : OddPerfect N) :
  ∃ p ∈ N.primeFactors, ∃ r ∈ N.primeFactors, p ≠ r ∧ 58 < p ∧ 58 < r

OddPerfect.exists_two_primeFactors_gt in OddPerfect.SecondLargestPrime

The sum of the reciprocals of the prime factors lies strictly between 23/40 and log 2, about 0.6931 (modules ReciprocalSum and ReciprocalLogTwo). The upper bound is a classical result.

theorem twentythree_fortieths_lt_sum_inv (h : OddPerfect N) :
  (23 / 40 : ℝ) < ∑ p ∈ N.primeFactors, 1 / (p : ℝ)

OddPerfect.twentythree_fortieths_lt_sum_inv in OddPerfect.ReciprocalSum

theorem sum_inv_lt_log_two (h : OddPerfect N) :
  ∑ p ∈ N.primeFactors, 1 / (p : ℝ) < Real.log 2

OddPerfect.sum_inv_lt_log_two in OddPerfect.ReciprocalLogTwo

Exponents are capped by the largest prime

known result formalized

If every prime factor is at most R, then every exponent e satisfies 2e + 3 ≤ R.

theorem two_mul_factorization_add_three_le (h : OddPerfect N) {p R : ℕ}
  (hp : p ∈ N.primeFactors) (hR : ∀ r ∈ N.primeFactors, r ≤ R) :
  2 * N.factorization p + 3 ≤ R

OddPerfect.two_mul_factorization_add_three_le in OddPerfect.ExponentCap

Proof idea. For an ordinary prime with exponent e, a primitive prime of order e + 1 divides σ(pe) and hence N, and it is ≡ 1 (mod 2(e + 1)), so it is at least 2e + 3. The special prime is handled with two primitive primes, of orders k + 1 and (k + 1)/2. Example: σ(3⁴) = 11², and 3 has order 5 modulo 11 = 2·4 + 3.

Certificate checker

tool

A small verified checker for factor chain arguments. A certificate that passes the check excludes every odd perfect number matching its root facts. No family of root facts is proved to cover every odd perfect number. The rules are described on the Methods page.

theorem check_sound {s : List Fact} {c : Cert} (hc : check s c = true) :
  ∀ N : ℕ, OddPerfect N → State.Holds s N → False

OddPerfect.Cert.check_sound in OddPerfect.Certificate

Proof idea. Induction on the certificate tree. A step rule adds a fact that holds for every odd perfect number satisfying the state, or splits into two exhaustive cases; each closing rule derives a contradiction from facts recorded in the state. The conclusion covers only odd perfect numbers that satisfy the root facts of the state.
